Three corrections before you study anything

Wrong target

There is no calculus on the ALEKS placement test.

The unit circle — memorize this quadrant only

Each point is (cos θ, sin θ). For any angle outside quadrant I: find its reference angle to the x-axis, read the value off this quadrant, then apply the sign from the ASTC letters. That's the whole method — no additional memorization.

Logarithms from zero

Also absent from your module, also on the test. One definition and five laws.

logb(x) = y  ⟺  by = x

Domain trap ALEKS loves: the argument of a logarithm must be strictly positive. For log(x − 3) the domain is x > 3, and any solution that makes an argument zero or negative gets discarded — always check candidate solutions back in the original equation.

Test-day rules

  1. Paper, pencil, 90 uninterrupted minutes. You cannot go back to a previous question, and the test adapts to what you've already shown. One sitting, no distractions.

  2. Use "I don't know" honestly. A wrong guess and an "I don't know" cost the same on that question, but a wrong guess can send the test probing down a branch you can't defend and waste questions you could have scored on. If you genuinely have no idea, say so. If you can make real progress, work it.

  3. Expect no calculator on most items. ALEKS supplies one only where it's allowed. Practice quadratics, radicals and exact trig values by hand all week.

  4. Answer in exact form. Radicals stay radicals. √2/2, not 0.707. Decimal approximations get marked wrong.

  5. Do it honestly. Placement is proctored, and beyond that, a score you didn't earn just drops you into a class that will bury you in week three.

The honest read, and the fallback

What this plan can and can't do

Repairing the algebra is very achievable in a week — that material is rust, and rust comes off fast. Learning trigonometry from absolute zero in two days is not learning trigonometry; it is pattern-matching hard enough to survive roughly six adaptive questions. That can be enough to clear 76 if the algebra carries the rest. It is a real shot, and it's worth taking, especially with two attempts.

What it won't do is make you ready for the class. MAT 265 uses trig constantly — derivatives of sine and cosine, trig limits, related-rates problems built on triangles, trig substitution later on. A student who crammed the unit circle for a placement test in August is generally in serious trouble by October.

The fallback is not a failure

If the score doesn't come: take MAT 170 (Precalculus) this fall and MAT 265 in spring. You'd learn trig properly, with a semester to absorb it, and walk into Calculus I actually prepared. It costs one semester on the math sequence, and for a CS degree that's usually absorbable — your first-semester programming courses don't depend on Calculus I.

Take the shot this week. Just decide now that the fallback is acceptable, so that a number below 76 on Sunday is a scheduling adjustment rather than a crisis.
